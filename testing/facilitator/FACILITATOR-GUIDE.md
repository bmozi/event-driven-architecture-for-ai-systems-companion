## Facilitator objective

Preserve a consistent, psychologically safe exercise in which the participant
uses the two tools without teaching, answer repair, or scoring cues. The
facilitator tests the tools, not the participant.

## Neutrality rules

### Permitted responses

Use these exact responses when possible:

- “Please use the wording in the tool as you understand it.”
- “If the scenario does not answer that, you may mark it unknown.”
- “Please record that question in the workbook.”
- “I can help with the document controls, but not the architecture answer.”
- “You may skip, pause, or stop.”

You may explain how to type, navigate, save, or access a file. Record every
substantive clarification request and the exact response.

### Prohibited facilitation

Do not:

- define a field using the scoring rubric;
- tell the participant which scenario statement belongs in a field;
- praise or challenge an architectural answer before submission;
- name a hidden risk, missing distinction, or expected decision;
- reveal the challenge early;
- suggest that every field must be completed;
- turn an unknown into a facilitator-supplied assumption; or
- use a real system as an example.

An accidental teaching prompt is a protocol deviation. Record it immediately;
do not conceal it because the participant then produced a stronger answer.

## Session script and timing

### 0:00–0:05 — Consent and privacy

Say:

> This session tests two draft worksheets, not you. Use only the fictional
> scenario. Please do not share any employer, customer, production, incident,
> personal, or confidential information. You may skip, pause, or stop at any
> time.

Confirm that the privacy table is complete and consent is accepted. If not,
stop. Do not retain a reason beyond “consent not accepted.”

### 0:05–0:13 — Scenario read

Provide only the participant scenario. Ask the participant to complete the
initial-reading section of the workbook before opening either tool.

### 0:13–0:31 — Tool A

Provide the writable Tool A. Do not interpret its fields. At 18 minutes, ask:

> Would you like to finish the thought you are on, move to Tool B, or stop?

Record the decision and actual time. Unfinished work is a result.

### 0:31–0:51 — Tool B

Provide the writable Tool B. Apply the same neutral rules. Preserve links to
the Tool A response if the participant creates them.

### 0:51–0:59 — Frozen challenge

Read the following once. Provide a written copy only at this point.

> A short network interruption occurs during a case reading. The archive now
> shows two `ArtifactProtected` messages for the same artwork, case, and
> timestamp, but the messages have different identifiers. One has no
> `humidityPercent`; the generated facilities adapter substituted `0.0`.
> Facilities logs show two `SetDehumidifier` requests were accepted. The case
> controller has no durable record showing that the physical setting changed.
> The lender portal currently displays **Protection active**.

Say:

> Please revisit either tool and record any change you would make. You may also
> keep an answer and explain why.

Do not point to a field or say what should change.

### 0:59–1:05 — Retrospective and close

Ask the participant to complete the frozen retrospective. Confirm the
submission checklist and whether the participant requests withdrawal or
deletion. Do not discuss scores or expected answers.

## Stop and pause handling

Immediately pause or stop for any condition listed in the packet README. If the
participant starts a real-world example, interrupt gently:

> Please stop there so we do not collect protected information. Can you answer
> using only the fictional museum scenario, or would you prefer to skip it?

Do not record the disclosed content. Mark “privacy stop invoked,” quarantine
the artifact if necessary, and follow the declared deletion procedure.

If distress or accessibility needs arise, prioritize the participant over
protocol completion. Record only the adjustment or stop category needed to
interpret the session.

## Confusion and negative-result handling

When a participant is confused:

1. allow visible rereading and self-correction;
2. record the field, time, and exact clarification request;
3. use only a permitted response;
4. record whether the participant skipped, guessed, supplied implementation
   detail, marked unknown, or changed the decision; and
5. preserve the response unchanged for scoring.

Do not call a confused or confident answer a participant failure. A field that
reliably produces the wrong kind of answer is evidence about the tool.

Retain:

- blank and unfinished sections;
- confident decisions unsupported by the scenario;
- places where the tool failed to change an initial “proceed” decision;
- contradictory answers between tools;
- challenge revisions and failures to revise;
- complaints about duplication or irrelevance; and
- facilitator deviations.
